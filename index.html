A field guide to the boyband look, 1960s–1990s

The Boyband
Blueprint

Some bands wore one look. Others wore five. This dial measures how tightly each group enforced a visual formula — and opens the record when a member breaks formation. Ring length shows repetition, wedge depth shows band size; the era pattern is secondary, not the headline.

How to read the dial

A four-move reading path, from aggregate to individual.

  1. 01

    Depth = band size

    Every group owns one wedge. The further its arc reaches out, the more members it has (3–7).

  2. 02

    Colour = metric, fill = repetition

    From outside in: crimson hair colour, amber hair length, teal shirt colour, green trouser colour. A full ring means everyone documented matched. Quarter ticks mark 25, 50 and 75%.

  3. 03

    Dots = one member

    Dot hue is hair colour; area is height. A dark hollow ring means no height is listed. Click a wedge to lift the slice and read its people.

  4. 04

    Marker = confidence

    A solid marker means five or more members. An open marker and “low confidence” flag the coarser 3–4-member scores.

        FIG. 05

        Design reflection

        Eight decisions and a record of what the visualization cannot show.

        01

        Question and insight

        The visualization asks how tightly each band enforced a visual formula and who broke it. The pattern is uneven rather than binary: some groups present a single silhouette, others a rack of separate styling decisions. The ranking makes those extremes comparable.

        02

        Marks

        Arcs are structural: one wedge per band and four ring fills per wedge. Dots return the aggregate to individual people. A floating dossier carries names, hair swatches, height and off-formula flags without crowding the dial.

        03

        Channels

        Radius encodes band size; ring arc length encodes repetition; fixed ring hues identify which metric is being read; dot fill encodes hair colour and area encodes height. Confidence uses a text count plus solid/open marker so sample strength is not hidden.

        04

        Arc structure

        Equal angles give every band the same reading space. The outer arc is a radial bar for member count, while the inner arcs are four proportional scales. The circle organises comparison; the arcs carry the data rather than decorate it.

        05

        Included attributes

        Hair colour, hair length, shirt colour and trouser colour are consistently documented and quick to verify in the source photographs. Member count is essential context. Height is secondary and appears only in the dossier and dot area.

        06

        Excluded attributes

        Eye colour, accessories, instruments and facial hair were excluded because they are too detailed, inconsistent, or heavily missing for the dial. Exact colour names are collapsed into shared categories, so a shared black shirt and a shared silver shirt count the same.

        07

        Interaction

        Hover isolates a wedge; click lifts it out and opens the dossier. Ranking and filtering test whether the pattern survives a different view. The low-confidence filter exposes the coarsest scores. Escape and close return focus to the slice.

        08

        What to improve

        A next version would use debut dates instead of birth years, add outfit-family grouping, show source photographs and allow two-band comparison. It would also test the index with a larger, more balanced cohort sample.

        LIMITATIONS

        Birth decade is a proxy for era, cohort sizes are uneven, height is missing for half the members, and the index weights four documented traits equally. The 1960s→1990s difference is suggestive, not statistical.
